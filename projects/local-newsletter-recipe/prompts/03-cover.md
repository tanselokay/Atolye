# Cover-art prompts

Covers are deliberately **metaphorical** — never screens, robots, code or logos. A plain everyday
scene carries the story's idea; the headline does the naming.

## The house style (appended to every cover prompt)

```
Bright, optimistic editorial illustration for a premium technology magazine. Soft early-morning
light — the warm golden light of dawn — high-key and airy, open sunlit space, a hopeful
first-light palette. Uplifting and forward-looking mood, spacious and clean, painterly texture,
elegant composition with a clear focal point, refined and intelligent rather than sensational.
No darkness, no night, no gloom.
```

> The dawn-light cue is a **signature**: the maker's name, *Tansel*, means the light of daybreak,
> so the covers carry it. Give yours a signature of its own.

## Proposing a scene

Ask a text model for 2–3 concrete everyday scenes that carry the story's *main idea* through a
situation a reader can map onto it (a coordinated effort, a careful check, a repaired structure,
a new tool waiting). Forbid text, logos, company names and "AI-looking" imagery. Drop anatomy and
literal comparisons — pick one concrete thing from the story's own world and paint that.

## Judging a cover

Show the generated image to a vision model with the headline and summary, and have it describe
what is **actually** painted (never what was intended): does the central scene carry the story's
main idea? Mark down contradictions, forbidden elements (text/logos), and malformed objects.
Keep the best; regenerate the rest.
